One day of tuning, same model, same card

Every number below is a paired A/B on this machine, run one GPU job at a time under a thermal and power watchdog, and gated on an 80K-token correctness check and a frozen 24-task coding suite before anything went into production. Seven rounds, three hardware findings, and a production configuration that reads long prompts 47% faster and writes 34 to 49% faster, with the vision encoder loaded and lower energy per token.

Before and after

Morning: engine 0.1.39, GPU capped at 100 W, prompt chunk 8192. Now: engine 0.1.43.1 with the Volta decode and prompt kernels and the async adaptive expert cache, 135 W, CPU boost capped at 4.5 GHz, chunk ceiling 32768, 12 workers, vision encoder loaded. Short prompts are 400-token coding answers at medium reasoning; the long prompt is a fresh 80K-token fixture with planted secrets that must come back verbatim.

Prefill is power-bound

80K prefill against the GPU cap

Engine 0.1.39 sweep, boost clocks; 135 W point is the final engine. Kernels and chunking moved prefill by 1 to 2%; watts move it by tens.

The 135 W soak

GPU power, five back-to-back 180K prefills with the prompt cache defeated; 13 minutes under load, 5/5 secrets, no watchdog stop.

Mean under load 133 W, p95 177 W, peak sample 229 W. The peaks are the load burst itself: capping the clock ceiling did not trim them, which is why 150 W was left alone on a PC that has lost power under GPU load.

The CPU was boosting for nothing

The 7950X pinned its 85 °C limit in every run of the day. Prefill is one user-space core on the host side of the GPU pipeline, and above 4.5 GHz its clock never reached the critical path. Capping boost at 4.5 GHz left every engine number alone and took 22 °C and 31% of the CPU's energy per generated token off the table. This closed the cooling question without new hardware.

Whole tasks, not just tokens

Decode speed can be bought with slower prefill. The frozen 24-task DeepSWE2 suite (seed 40, medium reasoning) measures the whole request: time to first token, generation, and the answer's correctness. October 13 ran at a 250 W cap on the old engine; the final run is at 125 W on the production configuration.

Median task wall 37.2 s → 29.6 s; passes 22/24 → 23/24 (two tasks flip in opposite directions, both known to vary between repeats). Median decode inside the tasks 77 → 92 tok/s; first token in 1.4 to 1.8 s either way.

What the kernel changed, and what it could not

Measured: nothing kernel-side on the engine's critical path

Identity IOMMU mapping and the NMI watchdog off, paired before and after a reboot: decode, prefill, 80K wall and CPU joules all within 1%. The PCIe probe was already at the Gen3 x16 practical limit (13.1 GB/s). The NVIDIA PAT option is inert on driver 580.

What did pay: the governor and the boost cap

power-profiles-daemon had pinned a performance profile; balanced brings idle CPU package power from 85 to 59 W with the model loaded. The 4.5 GHz cap above is the other half. Both are drop-ins that survive a reboot.

What did not move the needle

  • Locked SM clocks at any cap, and clock ceilings at 135 W (cost decode, peaks unchanged).
  • Worker count beyond 12, draft length 3, 5 or 6, KV window 64K, pool spin, ring size, larger n-gram row cache, swap and adaptation rates.
  • Two Volta decode opt-ins (argmax rows, fast norm), serving the shards from the Samsung 980 PRO, a second Volta prompt kernel (would conflict in 13 hunks for a projected 2 to 4% at 170 to 250 W).
  • Resident-biased routing: real per-token gain, worse per task (22/24, longer answers).
  • Pinning the engine to one chiplet: the expert streaming path wants both L3 caches.
  • Transparent huge pages (already on), suffix drafting (already default), the hybrid 4-bit KV cache (cannot stream).

What is left

  • BIOS. Restore AC Power Loss = Power On is the one setting that matters for a PC that drops power. AI Overclocking off and Curve Optimizer −20 → −10 or 0 are stability hygiene. With the boost cap in place, the PBO limits matter little.
  • Prefill. Still the long-prompt bottleneck (180K ≈ 2.8 min) and only watts move it. 150 W is the approved ceiling and was deliberately not probed.
  • Driver. Stay on 580 / CUDA 12. CUDA 13 dropped Volta.
